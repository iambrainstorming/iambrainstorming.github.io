# **Course Title:** Interdisciplinary Perspectives on Gender Equality: Biology, Psychology, and Economics
### **Subtitle:** *From Systemic Inequality to Teal, Cooperative, and Closed-Loop Economies*

### **Course Description**
This course examines the foundations, manifestations, and solutions to gender inequality through the integrated lenses of biology, psychology, and economics. It challenges biological determinism, explores the psychological mechanisms of gender socialization, and analyzes the economic structures that perpetuate disparities. Moving beyond traditional corporate and capitalist models, the course introduces **Teal organizational design, stakeholder-based closed-looped economics, and cooperative ownership** as structural remedies. By synthesizing these fields, students will learn to evaluate complex real-world issues and design regenerative, self-managing, and equitable systems tailored to the Indian context.

### **Learning Objectives**
By the end of this course, students will be able to:
1. **Biological Literacy:** Distinguish between empirical findings in neurobiology and popular myths of biological determinism, understanding how human biology thrives in symbiotic (cooperative) rather than purely competitive environments.
2. **Psychological Analysis:** Evaluate how gender identity develops and how implicit bias, stereotype threat, and social role expectations shape cognitive outcomes, and how "wholeness" in Teal organizations mitigates psychological burnout.
3. **Economic Evaluation:** Decompose gender disparities in labor markets and unpaid care work, and evaluate alternative economic models (closed-loop, stakeholder, and cooperative) that internalize care and environmental costs.
4. **Systemic Design & Policy Synthesis:** Design evidence-based interventions utilizing cooperative governance, Teal self-management, and circular economic principles to promote substantive gender equality in India.

### **Curriculum Structure (12-Week Module Breakdown)**

#### **Module 1: Foundations of Sex, Gender, and Equality (Week 1)**
- **Topics:** Defining sex, gender, and equality (formal vs. substantive equality); the history of gender studies; introduction to intersectionality (how gender intersects with caste, class, religion, and geography in India).
- **Key Question:** How do we define equality, and why is an interdisciplinary approach necessary to dismantle systemic bias?

- [Sex Determination](https://www.nature.com/scitable/topicpage/genetic-mechanisms-of-sex-determination-314/)
- [Are There Two Sexes? Yes and No, But Mostly No (and Gender is Something Else Anyway – More or Less)](https://journals.publishing.umich.edu/ptpbio/article/id/5905/)
- [Sexuality is polygenic](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/science/there-is-no-gay-gene-there-is-no-straight-gene-sexuality-is-just-complex-study-confirms)
- [Intersectionality in India: Analyzing Caste, Class, and Gender Dynamics](https://sociology.institute/sociology-of-gender/intersectionality-caste-class-gender-india/)

#### **Module 2: The Biology of Gender and Sex (Weeks 2–3)**
- **Topics:** Genetics, endocrinology, and brain structure; the "gender similarities hypothesis"; neuroplasticity and how environment shapes biology (debunking "biology is destiny"); 
- **Biological Metaphor for Equality:** Exploring biological *symbiosis and mutualism* in nature as the baseline for human survival, challenging the "survival of the fittest/most competitive" patriarchal narrative.
- **Readings:** Anne Fausto-Sterling (*Sexing the Body*); Cordelia Fine (*Delusions of Gender*).


- [Two minds The cognitive differences between men and women](https://stanmed.stanford.edu/how-mens-and-womens-brains-are-different/)
- [How Neuroplasticity Works](https://www.verywellmind.com/what-is-brain-plasticity-2794886)
- [How to Understand Symbiosis?: The Conflict and Integration of Two Pictures of Life](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-99-7325-5_8)

#### **Module 3: Psychological Dimensions of Gender in India (Weeks 4–5)**
- **Topics:** Developmental psychology of gender identity; cognitive psychology (meta-analyses on abilities); social psychology (implicit bias, stereotype threat). 
- **Indian Context:** Gender role development in joint vs. nuclear families; the psychology of "honor," dowry, and son preference; the psychological burden of unpaid care, domestic violence, and the stigma surrounding mental health seeking among Indian women.
- **Readings:** Janet Shibley Hyde; Alice Eagly; Claude Steele (*Whistling Vivaldi*); Malvika Chitnis (*Psychology of Gender in India*).

#### **Module 4: The Economics of Gender Inequality in India (Weeks 6–7)**
- **Topics:** Human capital theory vs. labor market discrimination; decomposing the gender wage gap; occupational segregation; the "motherhood penalty."
- **Indian Context:** 
  - **Labor Markets:** The U-shaped curve of Female Labor Force Participation Rate (FLFPR); the "income effect" vs. "substitution effect" in rural households.
  - **The Informal Sector:** Why 90%+ of working Indian women are in the informal sector; occupational segregation (Oaxaca-Blinder methodology).
  - **The Care Economy:** Analyzing the NSSO Time Use Survey (2019); the macroeconomic cost of unpaid domestic work.
- **Readings:** Claudia Goldin (*Career and Family*); Ashwini Deshpande (Indian wage gap studies).

#### **Module 5: Behavioral Economics, Norms, and Teal Organizational Design (Week 8)**
- **Topics:** Gender differences in risk aversion and negotiation—are these innate or context-dependent? 
- **Teal Organizations (Frederic Laloux):** Introduction to Teal paradigms (Self-Management, Wholeness, Evolutionary Purpose). 
  - *Psychology:* How "wholeness" (bringing one's full emotional/self identity to work) reduces the psychological masking burden disproportionately placed on women.
  - *Biology:* The biological toll (cortisol/stress) of traditional rigid hierarchies vs. the nervous-system benefits of self-managed, psychologically safe Teal structures.
- **Indian Context:** Contrasting traditional paternalistic Indian corporate hierarchies with emerging Indian startups adopting holacracy and self-management.

#### **Module 6: The Care Economy and Stakeholder-Based Closed-Looped Economics (Week 9)**
- **Topics:** Feminist economics; the valuation of unpaid labor; the "second shift."
- **Closed-Looped & Stakeholder Economics:** Moving from linear, extractive shareholder capitalism to regenerative, closed-loop (circular) economics. 
  - *Economics:* How closed-loop systems internalize externalities (like environmental degradation and unpaid care work) rather than offloading them onto women.
  - *Biology:* Designing economic systems that mimic biological ecosystems (zero waste, circular nutrient/resource flows).
- **Indian Context:** Women-led micro-enterprises in India's circular economy (e.g., waste-picker cooperatives, sustainable textile recycling, and organic farming networks).
- **Readings:** Kate Raworth (*Doughnut Economics*); Arlie Hochschild (*The Second Shift*).

#### **Module 7: Intersectionality, Development, and Cooperative Organizations (Week 10)**
- **Topics:** How gender inequality compounds with other identities; global case studies in development.
- **Cooperative Organizations:** The economics and psychology of worker-owned and multi-stakeholder cooperatives.
  - *Economics:* How democratic governance and profit-sharing structurally eliminate the gender wage gap and bypass the "glass ceiling."
  - *Psychology:* Shifting from individualistic competition to collective efficacy and shared leadership.
- **Indian Context (Deep Dive):** India’s powerful cooperative movement as a blueprint for gender equity. Case studies of **SEWA** (Self Employed Women's Association), **Lijjat Papad** (a massive women-owned cooperative), **Kudumbashree**, and Women Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs).
- **Readings:** Bina Agarwal (*A Field of One’s Own*); Elinor Ostrom (Governing the Commons).

#### **Module 8: Evidence-Based Policy, Interventions, and Future Systems (Weeks 11–12)**
- **Topics:** Evaluating what works: blind hiring, paternal leave quotas, subsidized childcare, and gender-responsive budgeting.
- **Designing the Future:** How to transition traditional Indian institutions (corporates, NGOs, government bodies) toward Teal, Cooperative, and Closed-Loop models.
- **Capstone Project:** Students draft an interdisciplinary policy or organizational design brief addressing a specific gender inequality issue, requiring them to cite biological, psychological, and economic evidence, and propose a structural solution using Teal, Co-op, or Closed-Loop principles.

### **Pedagogical Approaches & Assessment**
1. **Myth-Busting Labs:** Analyze popular media claims about gender and trace them back to primary scientific literature.
2. **Data Analysis:** Use Indian datasets (NFHS-5, PLFS, NSSO Time Use) to run regressions decomposing wage gaps or analyzing care work.
3. **Organizational Audits:** Students conduct a "Gender & Systems Audit" of a local Indian company or NGO, evaluating it against Teal principles (wholeness/self-management) and cooperative economics, and proposing a transition roadmap.
4. **Assessments:** 
   - *Midterm:* Interdisciplinary essay synthesizing biological and psychological perspectives on a chosen topic.
   - *Final:* A systemic design brief proposing a new Cooperative or Teal enterprise, or a Closed-Loop policy intervention, aimed at a specific Indian stakeholder (e.g., NITI Aayog, a State Government, or a Corporate Board).

### **Why This Expanded Model Works**
- **Biology** provides the baseline of human variation and proves that human beings are biologically wired for *symbiosis, neuroplasticity, and ecological balance* (closed-loops), not rigid hierarchies or extractive competition.
- **Psychology** explains the *mechanisms* of how societal norms are internalized, and proves that **Teal "wholeness"** and **Cooperative "collective efficacy"** are required to heal the psychological burdens of patriarchy and caste.
- **Economics** provides the *structural framework*. By shifting from shareholder primacy to **Stakeholder Closed-Loop Economics** and **Cooperative Ownership**, we structurally mandate that care work, environmental health, and gender equity are valued as core economic assets, not externalized costs. 

This curriculum ensures students graduate not just with theoretical knowledge of inequality, but with the **organizational and systemic blueprints** required to build a genuinely equitable, regenerative future in India.
