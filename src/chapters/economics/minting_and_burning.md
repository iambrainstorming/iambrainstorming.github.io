# The Simple Math of Printing and Burning Money

Date: 26-11-2026

> Most people find the concepts of printing or burning money confusing because they mistake money for actual wealth, when it is really just a measuring stick for real goods and services. The math behind it is beautifully simple: money and goods exist in a fixed ratio.


**Quantity Theory of Money**: if the amount of goods stays the same, changing the amount of money changes the price of those goods, which in turn changes everyone's purchasing power.

Let's build on the excellent foundation. We'll formalize the burning and minting examples into clear tables and explain them in detail.

---

## 🍎 The Baseline (Starting Point)

*   **Total Money Supply:** $1,000
*   **Total Goods:** 100 kg of apples
*   **Price per kg:** $1,000 / 100 kg = **$10/kg**

| Person | Money Held | Purchasing Power (kg of apples) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Person 1 | $200 | 20 kg |
| Person 2 | $300 | 30 kg |
| Person 3 | $100 | 10 kg |
| Person 4 | $400 | 40 kg |
| **Total** | **$1,000** | **100 kg** |

---

## 🔥 Part 1: Burning Money (Deflation)

When money is burned, the total supply shrinks. Because there are still 100 kg of apples, the price per kg **drops**. The remaining money becomes more valuable.

### Scenario A: Targeted Burn (Wealth Transfer)

$200 is burned *only* from Person 4's wallet.

*   **New Money Supply:** $1,000 − $200 = **$800**
*   **New Price:** $800 / 100 kg = **$8.00/kg**

| Person | Money After Burn | New Purchasing Power (kg) | Change in Purchasing Power |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Person 1 | $200 | $200 / $8.00 = **25.00 kg** | **+5.00 kg** (Gains) |
| Person 2 | $300 | $300 / $8.00 = **37.50 kg** | **+7.50 kg** (Gains) |
| Person 3 | $100 | $100 / $8.00 = **12.50 kg** | **+2.50 kg** (Gains) |
| Person 4 | $200 | $200 / $8.00 = **25.00 kg** | **−15.00 kg** (Loses) |
| **Total** | **$800** | **100 kg** | **Net Zero** |

*Takeaway:* Targeted burning acts as a **wealth transfer**. Person 4 lost money, but Persons 1, 2, and 3 gained purchasing power *without doing anything*, simply because the price of apples fell.

### Scenario B: Flat $50 Burn From Each Person (NOT Neutral)

Burning a flat $50 from every wallet does **not** keep purchasing power the same. It removes a larger percentage from small holders than from large holders, so purchasing power shifts.

*   **Total Burned:** $50 × 4 = **$200**
*   **New Money Supply:** $1,000 − $200 = **$800**
*   **New Price:** $800 / 100 kg = **$8.00/kg**

| Person | Money Held | $50 Burn | Money After Burn | New Purchasing Power (kg) | Change in Purchasing Power |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Person 1 | $200 | $50 | $150 | $150 / $8.00 = **18.75 kg** | **−1.25 kg** (Loses) |
| Person 2 | $300 | $50 | $250 | $250 / $8.00 = **31.25 kg** | **+1.25 kg** (Gains) |
| Person 3 | $100 | $50 | $50 | $50 / $8.00 = **6.25 kg** | **−3.75 kg** (Loses) |
| Person 4 | $400 | $50 | $350 | $350 / $8.00 = **43.75 kg** | **+3.75 kg** (Gains) |
| **Total** | **$1,000** | **$200** | **$800** | **100 kg** | **Net Zero** |

*Takeaway:* A flat dollar burn redistributes wealth. Small holders lose purchasing power and large holders gain it — even though the total purchasing power across all people stays at 100 kg.

### Scenario C: Proportional Burn (Neutral — Same Percentage for Everyone)

To keep purchasing power unchanged, each person must burn the same **percentage** (20%) of their holdings, not the same dollar amount.

*   **Total Burned:** $40 + $60 + $20 + $80 = **$200**
*   **New Money Supply:** $1,000 − $200 = **$800**
*   **New Price:** $800 / 100 kg = **$8.00/kg**

| Person | Money Held | 20% Burn | Money After Burn | New Purchasing Power (kg) | Change in Purchasing Power |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Person 1 | $200 | $40 | $160 | $160 / $8.00 = **20.00 kg** | 0 kg (Same) |
| Person 2 | $300 | $60 | $240 | $240 / $8.00 = **30.00 kg** | 0 kg (Same) |
| Person 3 | $100 | $20 | $80 | $80 / $8.00 = **10.00 kg** | 0 kg (Same) |
| Person 4 | $400 | $80 | $320 | $320 / $8.00 = **40.00 kg** | 0 kg (Same) |
| **Total** | **$1,000** | **$200** | **$800** | **100 kg** | **Net Zero** |

*Takeaway:* If money is burned **proportionally** from everyone, **relative purchasing power remains exactly the same**. The only thing that changes is the nominal price tag on the apples.

---

## 🖨️ Part 2: Minting Money (Inflation)

Minting is the exact opposite. When new money is created, the total supply grows. Because there are still only 100 kg of apples, the price per kg **rises**. Existing money becomes less valuable.

### Scenario A: Targeted Minting (The "Cantillon Effect")


Imagine the central bank (or a protocol) mints $200 and gives it *only* to Person 4.

*   **New Money Supply:** $1,000 + $200 = **$1,200**
*   **New Price:** $1,200 / 100 kg = **$12.00/kg**

| Person | Money After Mint | New Purchasing Power (kg) | Change in Purchasing Power |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Person 1 | $200 | $200 / $12.00 = **16.67 kg** | **−3.33 kg** (Loses) |
| Person 2 | $300 | $300 / $12.00 = **25.00 kg** | **−5.00 kg** (Loses) |
| Person 3 | $100 | $100 / $12.00 = **8.33 kg** | **−1.67 kg** (Loses) |
| Person 4 | $600 | $600 / $12.00 = **50.00 kg** | **+10.00 kg** (Gains) |
| **Total** | **$1,200** | **100 kg** | **Net Zero** |

*Takeaway:* This demonstrates the **Cantillon Effect**. The person who gets the newly minted money first (Person 4) gets to spend it before prices rise. Everyone else suffers inflation (their $200 now buys fewer apples), effectively acting as a hidden tax that subsidizes Person 4.

### Scenario B: Flat $50 Mint To Each Person (NOT Neutral)

Minting a flat $50 and giving it to every person does **not** keep purchasing power the same. It adds a larger percentage to small holders than to large holders, so purchasing power shifts.

*   **Total Minted:** $50 × 4 = **$200**
*   **New Money Supply:** $1,000 + $200 = **$1,200**
*   **New Price:** $1,200 / 100 kg = **$12.00/kg**

| Person | Money Held | $50 Mint | Money After Mint | New Purchasing Power (kg) | Change in Purchasing Power |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Person 1 | $200 | $50 | $250 | $250 / $12.00 = **20.83 kg** | **+0.83 kg** (Gains) |
| Person 2 | $300 | $50 | $350 | $350 / $12.00 = **29.17 kg** | **−0.83 kg** (Loses) |
| Person 3 | $100 | $50 | $150 | $150 / $12.00 = **12.50 kg** | **+2.50 kg** (Gains) |
| Person 4 | $400 | $50 | $450 | $450 / $12.00 = **37.50 kg** | **−2.50 kg** (Loses) |
| **Total** | **$1,000** | **$200** | **$1,200** | **100 kg** | **Net Zero** |

*Takeaway:* A flat dollar mint redistributes wealth in the opposite direction. Small holders gain purchasing power and large holders lose it — even though the total purchasing power across all people stays at 100 kg.

### Scenario C: Proportional Minting (Neutral — Same Percentage for Everyone)

To keep purchasing power unchanged, each person must receive the same **percentage** (20%) of their current holdings, not the same dollar amount.

*   **Total Minted:** $40 + $60 + $20 + $80 = **$200**
*   **New Money Supply:** $1,000 + $200 = **$1,200**
*   **New Price:** $1,200 / 100 kg = **$12.00/kg**

| Person | Money Held | 20% Mint | Money After Mint | New Purchasing Power (kg) | Change in Purchasing Power |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Person 1 | $200 | $40 | $240 | $240 / $12.00 = **20.00 kg** | 0 kg (Same) |
| Person 2 | $300 | $60 | $360 | $360 / $12.00 = **30.00 kg** | 0 kg (Same) |
| Person 3 | $100 | $20 | $120 | $120 / $12.00 = **10.00 kg** | 0 kg (Same) |
| Person 4 | $400 | $80 | $480 | $480 / $12.00 = **40.00 kg** | 0 kg (Same) |
| **Total** | **$1,000** | **$200** | **$1,200** | **100 kg** | **Net Zero** |

*Takeaway:* Just like proportional burning, proportional minting preserves everyone's relative share of the economy. The only difference is that the price tag on apples went up from $10 to $12.

## 💡 Summary: The 3 Golden Rules of Money Supply

1. **Money is a Claim on Goods, Not Wealth Itself:** Printing more money doesn't create more apples. It just changes the number of dollars required to claim an apple.
2. **Proportional Changes are Neutral:** If you mint or burn money equally across all wallets (same percentage for everyone), relative wealth stays exactly the same. Only the nominal prices change.
3. **Targeted Changes Redistribute Wealth:** This is the most misunderstood concept in economics.
   - *Targeted Burning* benefits those who **keep** their money (deflationary gain).
   - *Targeted Minting* benefits those who **receive** the new money first, at the expense of everyone else's purchasing power (inflationary tax / Cantillon Effect).
   - **Flat dollar changes are not neutral:** adding or subtracting the same dollar amount from everyone helps small holders and hurts large holders (or vice versa). Only equal percentages preserve purchasing power.

This simple math perfectly explains why crypto projects "burn" tokens (to increase the purchasing power of remaining holders) and why central bank "quantitative easing" (minting) often feels like it makes the rich richer and the poor poorer — the new money is rarely distributed proportionally.
