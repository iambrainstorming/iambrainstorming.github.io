## The Broken Economy and the Stagnant Wage
The current economy is completely broken. The fundamental promise that hard work leads to prosperity has been shattered. According to data from the Economic Policy Institute (EPI), from 1979 to 2020, net productivity rose by 61.8%, while the hourly pay of typical workers grew by only 17.5% after adjusting for inflation. 

No matter how many hours you work, no matter how productive you become, your wages do not rise. The wealth you create is not going to you; it is being extracted. We need UBI to correct this historic injustice and ensure that the people who actually build the economy get to live in it.

## The Myth of "Where Will the Money Come From?"
Critics of UBI immediately ask, "Where will the money come from?" This question reveals a fundamental misunderstanding of modern economics. They do not understand that money is a social construct, a shared fiction that we agree upon so that we can coordinate society. It is similar to our belief in institutions, or even our belief in God—it works because we collectively agree it works. Fiat currency has no intrinsic value; its value is derived entirely from our collective trust and the laws of the state.

It is not the mere supply of money that matters; it is its circulation. Wealth creation is not about the total amount of money available, but rather how many times a single bill changes hands. If a $100 bill circulates 10 times within a local community, it generates $1,000 in community income. 

UBI boosts the economy precisely because of this "velocity of money." Lower- and middle-income individuals have a high marginal propensity to consume. When they receive UBI, they spend it immediately on goods and services, directly supporting local businesses. Economic activity isn't determined simply by the stock of money; circulation, spending, production, and productivity matter. If $100 is spent repeatedly on newly produced goods and services, it can support much more than $100 worth of economic transactions. UBI acts as a massive economic multiplier.
